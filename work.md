WEBSITE BUILD PROMPT — Site-Wide Reframe: Remove Limiting Language and Widen BOA's Scope

CONTEXT
Bridge Online Academy is not a traditional school with a fixed age range or a single curriculum track. A learner can follow the full Nigerian curriculum, or come to BOA for a single subject or skill on its own (for example coding or video editing), at any age, with no upper cutoff. Much of the existing site was built around an older, narrower framing: a fixed age range (5 to 17) and a two-tier Primary/Secondary structure presented as the whole offering. That framing is now inaccurate and needs to be found and corrected across the entire codebase, not just in the sections already discussed.

YOUR JOB
Go through every page, component, and piece of copy in the site and identify anywhere the language or visual structure implies a limit that doesn't actually exist: a capped age range, a forced choice between only two programmes, wording that assumes every learner wants the full curriculum, or a layout that has no room for a learner who just wants one subject. Where you find this, make a judgment call on how to correct it using the same pattern already established in this project (see EXAMPLES ALREADY AGREED below), and apply that same quality of thinking to anything not explicitly covered here. Do not wait for a full spec of every sentence, use the established direction to make sensible calls, and flag anything you're genuinely unsure about rather than guessing on details that materially change a claim (pricing, specific credentials, specific outcomes).

THE CORE REFRAME (apply this lens everywhere)
Old framing: BOA is a school for children aged 5 to 17, split into Primary and Secondary.
New framing: BOA offers two ways to learn, the full Nigerian curriculum or a single subject/skill on its own, open to any age with no cutoff. The two-tier Primary/Secondary split should no longer appear as the site's main structure anywhere.

Test every sentence and every layout against this: does it accidentally say or imply "this is only for children," "this stops at 17," "you must pick Primary or Secondary," or "the full curriculum is the only way to learn here"? If yes, rewrite or restructure it using the pattern below.

EXAMPLES ALREADY AGREED (use these as the model for tone and structure elsewhere)

Hero: "Live, teacher-led learning built around what you actually need. Follow the full Nigerian curriculum, or pick up a single subject like coding or video editing, at any age, with structure, support and room to thrive online."

Subjects section intro: "Learn the full Nigerian curriculum, or just the subject you're here for. Whether your child needs Maths and English taught properly, or you want to pick up Coding, Video Editing or Creative Writing on their own, every subject is taught live by a specialist teacher."

Programmes page structure: replace the current two-column Primary/Secondary layout with two distinct offers:

1. Full curriculum programmes, structured ongoing teaching in the wider Nigerian curriculum, built around a child's actual level rather than a fixed year group. Age bands (5-10, 11 and up) can still appear here as practical detail, but not as the page's main organizing structure or headline label.
2. Individual subjects, pick up a specific subject or skill on its own (coding, video editing, an exam-prep subject, or anything else BOA teaches) without enrolling in the full programme.

FAQ "What ages do you teach?": "BOA teaches learners of any age. You can follow the full Nigerian curriculum, or come to us for one subject or skill on its own, coding and video editing are two examples. There's no fixed age cutoff either way."

New FAQ to add: "Do I have to enroll in the full curriculum? No. You can choose a single subject if that's what you need, the full curriculum isn't required to learn with BOA."

WHERE TO CHECK (go beyond this list if you find more)

- Home page: hero, stats row (check "Students learning" and similar stats aren't implicitly age-limited by surrounding copy), Subjects section, Programmes section, WhyUs section, About/Ms. Zika teaser, testimonials (check testimonial framing doesn't only feature school-age children), closing CTA
- Programmes page: full restructure per the pattern above, subject breakdowns should make clear which subjects are available as standalone options versus only within the full curriculum, if that distinction is real
- About page: check the founding story and mission copy for language that describes BOA as a children's school only, widen where needed without rewriting Ms. Zika's actual story
- Become a Teacher page: check whether standards and copy assume teachers only teach school-age children in the full curriculum, adjust if BOA also needs teachers for standalone skill subjects
- FAQ page: audit every existing answer, not just the age question, for the same limiting assumptions
- Nav, footer, and any card or button label anywhere that still says "Primary" or "Secondary" as a category name
- Enrollment form: check whether fields like "current class" make sense for someone applying for a single skill subject as an adult, and adjust the form logic if it currently assumes every applicant is a school-age child in the full curriculum, this may need a branch in the form (full curriculum vs individual subject) rather than a copy-only fix
- Any structured data, metadata, or SEO copy (page titles, meta descriptions) that states an age range or Primary/Secondary framing

DESIGN SYSTEM MUST STAY INTACT
None of this is a visual redesign. Keep every existing color token, component (Button, AnimateIn, SectionLabel, CheckCircle, ChecklistCard, RegisterCTA, etc.), spacing scale, and animation pattern exactly as they are. Where a layout genuinely needs to change (for example the Programmes page moving from a two-column Primary/Secondary layout to a two-track full-curriculum/individual-subject layout), rebuild it using the existing component patterns and visual language, not a new design direction. The site should look like the same site before and after this pass, only the framing and structure of the content should change.

COPY STANDARD (carried over from before)
No AI slop, no em dashes, no generic filler like "unlock your potential" or "seamless experience." Every claim needs to be concrete. Where you rewrite a section, keep the same voice already established in the examples above, direct, warm, specific, not corporate.

WHAT TO AVOID

- Do not remove real, useful detail like age bands or subject breakdowns, this is a reframe, not a simplification
- Do not invent programmes, subjects, credentials, or outcomes that aren't already documented elsewhere in the project, when in doubt about a specific factual claim, flag it rather than guess
- Do not introduce a new rigid category to replace Primary/Secondary, the whole point is that the site should read as open, not swap one fixed label for another
- Do not touch the visual design system, layout components, or animation patterns beyond what's needed to fit the new content structure
- Do not leave any page, component, or piece of copy unchecked, if something wasn't explicitly listed above, use the core reframe test on it anyway

WHEN YOU FINISH
Provide a short summary of every file you changed and, for anything where you made a judgment call not explicitly covered in this prompt, a one-line note on what you decided and why, so it can be reviewed quickly rather than re-read in full.
